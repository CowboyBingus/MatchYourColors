# v1.5

- New option, Recolor Weapons (Off by default): your primary, sidearm and support weapon take your colors the way the game's weapon paint schemes repaint a weapon, body, details and trims, attachments and magazines included. The colors are the ones your helmet and armor take (the armor's, the helmet's or the cape's), with a camo when those colors include one.
- With one of the game's weapon paint schemes picked as Paint Scheme and Recolor Weapons on, your weapons wear that scheme exactly as the game paints it.
- In the Armory, the weapon you look at and the WEAPON CUSTOMIZATION preview show your weapon colors too.
- Squadmates who use the mod with Recolor Weapons on see each other's weapons in their colors.
- Sync With Mod Users now shares the settings in a form new options can extend without a new lobby value; squadmates on v1.3 and v1.4 still see your helmet, armor and cape settings.
- New option, Recolor Squadmates: Squadmates Without the Mod recolors squadmates who don't use this mod with your settings, on your screen only; Everyone does it for every squadmate. Off by default.
- New option, Squadmates' Colors: Their Own Gear (default) applies your Color Matching to those squadmates' own gear; Your Colors gives their helmet, armor, cape and weapons your colors (those of your armor, helmet or cape, as your Color Matching matches), so the squad matches you.
- New option, Hide Cape: Your Cape hides your cape on the ship and in missions, also on the screens of squadmates who use this mod; Everyone's Capes hides every squadmate's cape on your screen too. The Armory and the loadout screen still show capes, so you can pick one. Off by default.
- Fixed: in a squad of three or more, after a squadmate died, their weapons and another squadmate's could swap colors or stop being recolored. Each squadmate's weapons now keep their own colors through deaths and respawns.
- Match Materials now also works the other way: a hard painted part that takes a helmet's or armor's bare metal becomes that metal, so the B-22 Model Citizen's chrome and the AD-26 Bleeding Edge's cobalt show as chrome and cobalt on the other item, not as matte paint. Cloth never turns into metal, and a part that was paint is no smoother than the game's usual metal: mirror chrome stays on the parts made for it.
- With Helmet and Armor Match Cape and Match Materials, a cape's metallic color carries its metal too: under the Eye of Liberty your helmet's and armor's hard parts turn gold metal and the clothing takes the cape's black, instead of pale yellow cloth.
- With Helmet and Armor Match Cape and Match Materials off, a mostly one-color helmet or armor keeps its metal: the B-22 Model Citizen helmet's chrome under the Eye of Liberty is now a dark chrome instead of matte cloth.
- With Helmet and Armor Match Cape, your gear now matches the cape as it looks in the game, not its lighter base color: under the default black capes such as Foesmasher the armor is black, not grey.
- A light glossy part that a cape's cloth turns dark takes a matte finish, as the game's own dark paints have, instead of shining grey.
- Glossy parts and cloth are never painted darker than the game's own darkest paints of their kind: a part that cannot get darker under its own shine no longer turns a pitch-black paint.
- An armor that would turn one color takes the helmet's (or cape's) second color on its clothing, a dark color on its straps and pouches and the accent on its trims, all from the helmet's or cape's own colors: the SA-25 Steel Trooper no longer turns one solid color, its undersuit takes the helmet's dark. A helmet or cape without a second color of its own (one covering a tenth of it, or a twentieth for black or dark grey) leaves the armor as before, rather than a made-up shade.
- An armor's clothing never takes a helmet's or cape's bare metal as its second color: the B-01 Tactical with the Cowboy Hat takes the hat's dark brown leather, not the silver of its badge.
- Prosthetic arms and legs (SA-12 Servo-Assisted, SA-25 Steel Trooper, SA-32 Dynamo) take the helmet's or cape's dark color, or keep their own, and never the plates' color.
- A squadmate's colors stay ready while they are dead: when they respawn in the same gear they are recolored at once, without a new recolor.
- Modded helmets and armor whose colors are painted into their texture instead of the game's color table, such as the Cowboy Hat mod, now give their colors and take yours: the mod reads up to 8 colors from that texture as they look in the game and recolors a copy of it, keeping its shading, grain and metal.
- A modded material without a color table that the mod cannot read (another texture format) is now named in the log with the reason, instead of being left out silently.
- A cape design drawn light on dark (or dark on light) keeps a light-and-dark contrast on its new colors: Proof of Faultless Virtue on the UF-50 Bloodhound shows a silver design on red instead of a dark red one, and the Judgment Day's bands turn silver there too. When none of your colors does, it takes a light or dark grey of its new background: Proof of Faultless Virtue and Botslayer keep their designs with the FS-38 Eradicator's olive.
- Cape designs: a recolored decal sheet is only reused for a sheet of the same layout, and the data it held stays in memory until the game is done with it. The log now says for each cape design whether it was made or reused, to track down reports of garbled designs.
- The log's recolor lines now count reused textures too ("N new, M refilled textures").
- A helmet's or armor's vivid color that covers a tenth of it or more, but less of it than its main color, now reaches the other item's parts: the FS-05 Marksman's red shows on the FS-37 Ravager's plates and the I-92 Fire Fighter's orange on the B-22 Model Citizen's chrome. Other vivid colors stay accents, such as the PH-9 Predator's red beret on the I-44 Salamander's shoulder pad, and clothing never takes them.
- Clothing never takes the flat paint of a colored metal such as gold: it takes the other item's nearest plain color, so the BP-77 Grand Juror's gold no longer turns the B-24 Enforcer's coat mustard.
- Fixed since v1.4: a large colored part of an armor no longer takes a small accent of the helmet or cape: with the RS-100 Sanctioner the O-44 Bonded Pilot's red-brown plates are black again and its gold ropes gold, instead of light gold plates.
- Fixed: brown leather, tan and khaki parts took a helmet's or armor's accent color as a main color: with the PH-9 Predator the CPG-48 SAPPER's gloves, boots and waist panel turned bright red; they now take its olive, and only the shoulder pads its red.
- Fixed: an armor could stay one solid color when a band of its plates took the helmet's main color: the CE-35 Trench Engineer turned the SA-04 Combat Technician all orange; its plates now keep the orange and its suit takes the helmet's dark grey.
- A helmet's lighter minor color no longer covers most of an armor's clothing: with the FS-23 Battle Master the DP-00 Tactical's coat keeps the helmet's grey instead of its light grey.
- Fixed: on the loadout screen's squad cards, your Helldiver now shows your colors and each squadmate's shows theirs (or yours with Recolor Squadmates); before, a squadmate's card could take your colors while yours kept its own.
- Fixed: squadmates were never recolored in missions, because the mod looked for them only among your own player's records; it now finds every player in the squad, and one it cannot match to the lobby takes your settings with Recolor Squadmates on Everyone (or on Squadmates Without the Mod while nobody else shares settings).
- Fixed: in the Armory, a cape could keep its own colors, or show only part of its new colors, when you changed the armor or helmet under the same cape; it is now recolored as soon as the game has finished setting it up.

# v1.4

- New: Color Matching has a fourth choice, Helmet and Armor Match Cape: your helmet and armor take the colors of the cape you wear (its cloth, lining and emblems), and the cape keeps its own; in the Armory they follow the cape you preview.
- With Helmet and Armor Match Cape, dark gloves, boots and straps stay dark instead of taking a cape's light color.
- With Helmet and Armor Match Cape, an armor or helmet of mostly one color, such as the B-01 Tactical, now shows the cape's colors by material: plates its main color, clothing and trims its second, gloves, straps and padding its darkest (the Thundering Hooves of Loyalty made the whole B-01 red).
- New: a SEAF paint scheme in the game's own SEAF trooper colors, matched against SEAF troopers in the game: plates and helmet shells slate blue, clothing grey, and vests, straps, pouches, padding, gloves, boots and colored visors black or dark.
- Camo patterns now turn into shades of their new color: a green camo matched to a yellow helmet or cape no longer shows lime or lavender patches, nor cyan or lilac ones with a blue color.
- Squadmates on v1.3 see your gear in its own colors while you use Helmet and Armor Match Cape, and with your Color Matching instead of the SEAF scheme.
- Your Helldiver is recolored the frame after it appears instead of 8-13 frames later: the analysis cache and the plan for the gear you wear are prepared while the game loads.
- Items shown in the Armory for the first time are recolored about twice as fast (helmets about three times): the mod keeps its reader of the game's files open while you browse and keeps small pieces it already decoded, so far fewer file reads make it wait a frame.
- The textures a recolor needs are now made while the game loads (75 in under 4 ms on a loading frame), so no recolor on the ship or in the Armory has to make one during play (v1.3 made 36 at five recolors in the same test session).
- Lighter per frame: in the same scripted session on the ship and in the Armory, no frame of the mod's work went over 2 ms outside loading (v1.3: one of 2.2 ms), applying a recolor took at most 1.5 ms (v1.3: 2.1 ms), and browsing armors and helmets took about half the mod's time.
- In 7 minutes of real play on the ship and in the Armory, applying a recolor took 0.56-0.61 ms at the median and 1.33 ms at most, and no texture was made during play; per-frame cost in real play is not measured yet.
- A recolor's last step now has a frame of its own, a new look is bound straight over the old one instead of restoring the original textures first, and the game-file reader is closed a few files at a time instead of all on the frame of a recolor.
- Switching back to a look you had before is quicker: its job takes about 0.1 ms instead of 0.3 ms, as the kit records are read in fewer reads and their checksums are kept.
- Items and capes seen for the first time take less work, with exactly the same colors: color fitting does about 45% less work and a cape's analysis about 20% less.
- Fixed: with a LUT mod that saves its colors as 32-bit float textures (such as a DP-11 recolor and some HD helmet LUTs), every recolor matching to or from its helmet or armor failed ("unsupported texture format 2"), as did paint schemes such a mod replaced; these textures are now read as they are.
- Armor Matches Helmet with a bare-metal helmet such as the CPR-80 Bulwark no longer paints the armor a much lighter grey than the helmet looks: a part taking a metal's color is now as dark as the game's own matching sets paint it (the Bulwark's steel gives about L 29 instead of 39), less so for dark metals.
- A part that is darker than the main part taking the same color now stays somewhat darker, so straps, undersuits and dark plates no longer all turn the same light color as the plates.
- A helmet's bright stripe or trim color no longer paints large muted parts of the armor: the CPR-80 Bulwark's yellow stays on trims instead of turning the RS-40 Beast of Prey's leg camo or the DS-191 Scorpion's vest solid yellow.
- Shirts, undersuits and gloves never take such a stripe color, whatever their size: the PH-202 Twigsnapper's t-shirt and the RS-40's gloves no longer turn yellow with the Bulwark.
- A colored band or stripe that would turn black or grey now takes the other item's accent color: the IX-VOIDWALKER's navy band becomes red on the RE-1861 Parade Commander, lemon yellow on the B-01 Tactical and gold on the RE-2310 Honorary Guard, and a B-01 Tactical helmet's white stripe takes the yellow of the KDM-763 Pacesetter's light strips while its red turns black.
- Such a band is now one even coat of the other item's own accent paint, so it reads as the same color: the IX-VOIDWALKER's band on the B-01 Tactical is the armor's lemon yellow instead of a pale butter yellow.
- Smaller parts and printed stripes in a band's color take the band's new color too: a B-01 Tactical helmet's white dome stripes now turn yellow with its white jaw on the KDM-763 Pacesetter instead of staying white, while its silver badges stay metal.
- Metal parts that darken keep their shine instead of turning pitch black: the B-22 Model Citizen's chrome knee, shin and forearm guards under a black cape become dark chrome, one even paint across all of them.
- A part whose color the mod could only reach with an extreme paint no longer turns pale: the I-44 Salamander's metal plates under the Light of Eternal Liberty cape are its yellow instead of a pale cream.
- Colors are matched as paint, with shine apart, the way the eye sees them: a glossy helmet no longer turns darker than the grey it matches (a white B-01 Tactical helmet on the CM-14 Physician now takes the armor's grey instead of a near-black).
- A matte part no longer turns pale where it matches a glossy color: the SC-30 Trailblazer Scout's coat now takes the AF-50 Noxious Ranger mask's olive instead of a light grey.
- Muted colors keep their hue: a softly tinted color is now fitted for its tint too, so the SC-30's olive coat no longer drifts to khaki under the Armory's warm light, without ever turning more saturated than asked.
- Leather and cloth that show a warm or cool cast in the game now cancel it when they turn black or grey: the B-24 Enforcer's coat under a black cape is dark grey instead of rust brown.
- Bare metal that turns dark now stays a dark steel instead of a black mirror: the CPR-80 Bulwark under a black cape no longer looks pure black beside its armor.
- Cloth that turns a dark color deeper than its own sheen allows now keeps that color's hue: the UF-16 Inspector's red sleeve under a navy cape turns navy instead of purple.
- A two-tone source now shows both its colors on armors of two tones too: under the Thundering Hooves of Loyalty the SR-64 Cinderblock turns red with gold plates instead of solid red.
- With Helmet and Armor Match Cape, a cape of one main color and one bright color shows both on your armor: under a black cape with a red quarter the FS-37 Ravager gets red plates instead of turning all black.
- With Helmet and Armor Match Cape, the cape's outside now decides your outfit's main color, not its lining: under a black cape with a grey lining your armor turns dark, not grey.
- With Helmet and Armor Match Cape, a tinted color that shows only on a cape's lining no longer paints large parts: the EX-00 Prototype X's dome takes Standard of Safe Distance's sage panel instead of its tan lining.
- With Helmet and Armor Match Cape, a cape of essentially one color now also repaints your helmet and armor's own colored parts, so the outfit reads as the cape: the I-92 Fire Fighter's orange turns dark under a dark grey cape.
- With Helmet and Armor Match Cape, such a cape also repaints printed stripes and decals: the FS-23 Battle Master's yellow stripes turn the cape's grey.
- With Helmet and Armor Match Cape, a cape's second main color now shows on an all-dark armor too: under a black and yellow cape the BP-32 Jackboot gets yellow panels instead of staying all black.
- With Helmet and Armor Match Cape, undersuits take a cape color when the cape has no dark neutral color: the IE-3 Martyr's olive shirt and brown trousers turn the blue of Federation's Embrace.
- A black or grey color no longer takes on a part's own colored sheen as paint: the FS-05 Marksman's red dome under a black cape turns dark grey instead of teal.
- With Helmet and Armor Match Cape, a large brightly colored part no longer keeps its color just because the cape has a thin trim of a similar shade: the TR-7 Ambassador of the Brand's cyan plates turn black under a black cape with a thin navy hem.
- A part's light grey details now darken with it when its paint turns much darker.
- Light armor parts no longer take a helmet's small dark color when the armor's undersuit already has it: the SC-30 Trailblazer Scout's tan plates take the AF-50 Noxious Ranger's olive instead of black.
- A light glossy part matched to a dark color now takes the matte finish and the charcoal shade of the dark part it matches, as the game's own dark paints have: the IX-VOIDWALKER's shell on the B-01 Tactical no longer shows its scuffs as grey-white marbled grime, and white helmets such as the CW-36 Winter Warrior no longer turn flat black.
- Bare-metal armor plates matching a metal helmet are now as dark as matte parts matching it, so the PH-202 Twigsnapper's steel plates no longer look brighter than the Bulwark's steel.
- Fixed: clothing on parts that also show skin was never recolored: the PH-9 Predator's and PH-56 Jaguar's camo shirts, the IE-57 Hell-Bent's vest and scarf, and the sleeves, collars and gloves of the RS-40 Beast of Prey, O-2 Heavy Operator and PH-202 Twigsnapper; they now match like the rest, and bare skin keeps its color.
- Fixed: parts the game draws from a blank spot of their color mask were never recolored: the PH-56 Jaguar's shin wraps and the CM-21 Trench Paramedic's chest and shin pouches now match like the parts around them, as do small parts of the CPH-26 Commandant, CM-17 Butcher, CM-10 Clinician and CM-09 Bonesnapper.
- Fixed: with another mod changing an armor's parts, helmet accents could turn pure green, a hidden placeholder color in the game's data; parts the game never shows no longer give colors, and modded gear keeps its measured paint looks.
- With many mods installed, new items are recolored far sooner: the mod checks modded items against only the bytes it reads (never a modded mesh), reads only each mod's table of contents at the first recolor, and reads a large modded ID mask at a 512 x 512 mip.
- The Armory preview never shows an item you already moved away from: the item shown is read again just before its colors are applied, and work for an item you left stops early.
- New textures are made less often in the Armory: when some must be made, enough for the next item of the same kind are made in that same frame.
- The first recolor of a session no longer has a 4-5 ms frame reading the analysis cache, and a cache save no longer has a 3-4 ms frame.
- Less repeated work: a cape is not recomputed when only the other item changes, a paint scheme recomputes only the item that changed, and an emblem sheet still in use is not copied again.
- A squadmate's respawn with the same gear is recolored at once without a new job, and squadmates waiting for a recolor take turns.
- With Color Matching off and no paint scheme the mod reads one value per frame and does nothing on a respawn.
- Setting an option to the value it already has starts no recolor; the log lists all options in one line at startup.
- The Armory preview's animation alone no longer rebinds its colors.
- Large modded pattern masks without mip levels and long runs in the game's compressed data no longer make a single long frame.
- A pause or stop during a recolor no longer leaves game files open.
- Recolor plans kept in a session are capped at 128, the least recently used dropped first.
- The log names the texture and the mod's file when a texture cannot be read, and each recolor line gives the longest frame of its work.
- The disk cache starts over once (format 12).

# v1.3

- An armor that shows almost nothing but its dark suit, such as the SC-34 Infiltrator, now gives helmets its dark color instead of turning their base white from a small light-grey trim; the same for 11 other suits such as the RE-1861 Parade Commander, CE-07 Demolition Specialist and DP-00 Tactical, so an RS-100 Sanctioner's hood no longer turns white or red on them.
- New option Recolor Hoods (on by default): turned off, the hood of a hooded helmet (RS-100 Sanctioner, AC-2 Obedient, DS-10 Big Game Hunter, IE-12 Righteous and the SC-30 Trailblazer Scout helmets) keeps its own color like the visor, while its mask and trims still match.
- New option Match Materials (off by default): turned on, a bare-metal part that takes a painted color becomes that paint, so a chrome, steel or gold helmet turns white or green with a white or green armor instead of staying bronze or gold.
- Match Materials changes metal parts of at least 5% of the item and metal accents; small metal details such as bolts and rims, cloth, and painted parts keep their material, and nothing is turned into metal.
- New option Paint Scheme (Off by default): paints your helmet and armor in one of the game's 11 weapon paint schemes (Helldiver, Forest, Forest Camo, Arctic, Arctic Camo, Desert, Desert Camo, Urban, Urban Camo, Night, Venus); the camo schemes bring their camo pattern.
- Squadmates who use the mod see each other's helmet and armor in their chosen colors (Sync With Mod Users, always on, no option); only the settings travel, as a value of the squad's online lobby.
- The log notes a squadmate who shares settings but whose Helldiver the mod cannot find.
- Fixed: sharing your settings could crash the game a few seconds after reaching the ship, when a memory buffer the online lobby reads had been freed; settings are now shared only while your Helldiver exists and once the lobby has been stable for ten seconds.
- Helmets and armors changed by other mods (LUT edits such as recolored helmets and glowing visors) are now recolored from their modded colors; the mod read the game's original files and put the original colors back on modded items.
- Glowing parts keep their colors like visors and lights: the visors of visor-glow mods, and the game's own small light strips (such as on the PH-56 Jaguar and GS-66 Lawmaker), which also no longer pass their color to the other item.
- New option Recolor Cape (off by default): your cape takes the same colors as your recolored helmet or armor, or the paint scheme's, so a red lining such as the Cover of Darkness's no longer stands out.
- With Recolor Cape on, a complete set that Leave Complete Sets keeps as it is still gives its colors to your cape.
- Sync With Mod Users shares the Recolor Cape setting, so squadmates' capes follow their own settings.
- Recolor Cape also recolors a cape's own tint, the color its cape texture blends into the cloth, so United in Equality's purple band and the tinted parts of 40 other capes take the matched colors instead of keeping theirs.
- Recolor Cape keeps emblems, marks and sashes readable: one that would blend into the cloth around it takes the matched color that stands out most against it, or keeps its own, so United in Equality's emblem turns dark on its now light band with the CW-36 Winter Warrior.
- Recolor Cape keeps a cape's marks readable when your colors are equally dark: a mark that no color of yours sets apart by lightness takes one that stands out by its color, else a smaller color of yours, before keeping its own. On the UF-50 Bloodhound the Pillars of Freedom's red bars turn black on the red cloth, the Judgment Day's bands and Fre Liberam's eagle turn red, and Liberty's Herald's chevron turns silver.
- Recolor Cape reads which part of a cape shows which color in the order the game draws it (red before green before blue), which shifts a few capes slightly.
- Fixed: with Recolor Cape on, every recolor failed after a restart once the cape had been analysed in an earlier session.
- Fixed: Armor Matches Helmet no longer turns most of the CM-14 Physician yellow with the RS-67 Null Cipher: a colored part counts as a trim only while all parts of its color together stay under 15%, so the green follows the helmet's main colors.
- A part bigger than a tenth of the item takes the other item's accent color only when that item shows its accent on at least 1% of itself, so the RS-67 Null Cipher's speck of yellow no longer paints a whole armor part or a cape's band.
- A smaller colored part takes such a speck of accent only when it is bigger than the speck in the part's own hue family, so the CM-14 Physician's red canisters take the RS-67 Null Cipher's dark instead of its 0.3% yellow.
- A light neutral painted pattern takes the other item's neutral color nearest in lightness when that item has two or more, so the CM-14 Physician's cream panels turn the RS-67 Null Cipher's grey instead of staying cream.
- Recolor Cape keeps the emblems printed from a cape's emblem sheet readable: one that would blend into the cloth takes the color of yours that stands out most, in a copy of the sheet with only that emblem recolored, so the Drape of Glory's dark grey emblem turns light grey on the B-01 Tactical's dark cloth.
- Recolor Cape fits a cape's cloth to your color as it shows in the game, its sheen included: a cape cloth takes the measured look of armor cloth with the same material settings, so the Drape of Glory's suede no longer turns brown where your armor is near-black.
- The log notes, once per cape plan, how much of the cape its tint covers, what the emblem rules changed (design zones and emblem-sheet marks) and the largest color fit error.
- The disk cache starts over once (format 9).
- The disk cache starts over once when the installed mods that replace textures, materials or units change.
- A part's new color never ends farther from the asked color than the part's own color was: the color fit now starts from the part's own color (in the tests 126 of 1,865 fitted parts had ended farther, the worst by 11 CIEDE2000).
- A metal part no longer turns a deeper color than the other item shows: its paint stays as muted as the asked color, since a metal's reflections show its paint at full strength (the CPR-80 Bulwark turned deep blue with the DP-8 Mountain-Scaled's dull navy).
- Armor Matches Helmet no longer turns a whole armor a helmet's bright minority color: when a helmet's largest part is a vivid color on less than half of it, such as the PH-9 Predator's red beret, the armor takes the helmet's muted color and the vivid one goes to its accents (also two B-01 Tactical helmet variants and the AF-02 Haz-Master).
- A near-neutral color such as silver, grey or black passes on its lightness without its faint tint, so the GS-66 Lawmaker's silver no longer turns the B-24 Enforcer's brown leather navy-purple or its grey plates steel-blue.
- A bare-metal part that would take a dark color takes the other item's bare metal instead when it has some, so the CPR-80 Bulwark's steel echoes the DP-8 Mountain-Scaled's silver chainmail instead of turning near-black.
- A gold or other colored metal trim counts as an accent by how it looks, so the RE-2310 Honorary Guard's gold band takes the CE-27 Ground Breaker's yellow trim instead of turning black (also the O-44 Bonded Pilot).
- When the other item has no vivid accent but a second color, accents take the color the matching gives them instead of keeping their own: the AF-02 Haz-Master's yellow suit turns the IX-VOIDWALKER's cream and the DP-40 Hero of the Federation's gold trims its navy.
- Small accents and painted patterns take that second color, so the DP-00 Tactical's yellow trim turns the CW-36 Winter Warrior's dark instead of staying yellow, and the B-01 Tactical helmet's blue band the B-22 Model Citizen's dark.
- An item of one color, such as the all-dark AC-2 Obedient, still leaves accents their own color, since its color would erase them.
- A colored trim on a black or dark grey item counts as an accent up to 15% of what shows (10% before), so the DP-53 Savior of the Free's gold braid takes the B-01 Tactical helmet's yellow stripe instead of turning black.
- An armor that shows almost nothing but its suit, such as the O-3 Free Spirit, takes the helmet's colors on its suit instead of barely changing (7 armors, among them the SC-34 Infiltrator and one of the B-01 Tactical armors).
- A camo color of another hue than its part's paint takes the new hue instead of turning with the paint, so the TG-8 Sharpshooter's camo turns maroon with the UF-50 Bloodhound instead of a purple neither item has (also the CE-64 Grenadier).
- Painted stripes and other patterns asked for a color far from their own keep the asked hue: the B-01 Tactical's yellow stripes turn the IX-VOIDWALKER's navy where they showed grey-green, and stripes asked for grey or black take no tint.
- Cloth reaches the asked color with its sheen counted, so the TG-8 Sharpshooter's and O-3 Free Spirit's cloth turn the UF-50 Bloodhound's red instead of a lighter salmon pink; glossy leather keeps its highlights out, as before.
- The seven pairs from manual testing, and the DP-00 Tactical with the UF-50 Bloodhound that was already right, are pinned in the tests and were checked on screen in the Armory on both body types from four sides; an independent review of those renders cleared them.
- A part whose shine, metal or other finish another mod changed is no longer matched by how the original part looked in the Armory: its look is worked out from its new material, while a part whose colors alone a mod changed keeps the measured look.
- A helmet or armor whose units or ID masks another mod replaced uses the earlier model of what shows instead of the Armory measurement, which no longer fits its parts.
- The disk cache checks what each saved analysis read from other mods' files and makes it again when one changed, also when a large texture such as an ID mask was replaced by one of the same size (before, only the first 64 KB of each was compared).
- Transmog mods that rebuild an item at runtime under another item's id: each recolor reads the item's record again, so a rebuilt item gets a new recolor instead of one made for its old parts, also after a respawn and in the Armory.
- An item a transmog mod rebuilt with another item's parts takes that item's Armory measurement when exactly one measured item has those parts.
- The log names the options each recolor used (hoods kept, materials matched, cape).
- Tested offline on all 42,660 pairs with each option, with the Infiltrator's three pairs, four RS-100 Sanctioner pairs and three metal helmets pinned in the tests; Paint Scheme, Sync With Mod Users (through the real lobby, with the test build's own value) and two LUT mods checked in the game in a test session; Sync With Mod Users played between a host and a client, each seeing the other's colors.
- Recolor Cape tested offline on all 118 capes (rows, tints, design zones, 85 emblem-sheet marks and 46 recolored sheets equal the reference) and checked on screen in the Armory on 23 outfits from the front and behind; an independent review of the renders cleared them; not yet played.
- A game-data read the disk is slow to answer no longer holds up the game's frame: the recolor work waits a frame instead (real play saw single reads of 62 and 142 ms).
- Working out new colors pauses more often, inside each color fit too, so it keeps closer to its 1.5 ms per frame (outside the game the longest stretch without a pause fell from 0.78 to 0.45 ms).
- When a recolor's work used most of its frame, the recolor is applied on the next frame, so the two no longer add up in one frame.
- The disk cache is read and written in pieces over several frames instead of in one (saves took up to 5 ms in one frame in real play).
- Finding the installed mods' patch files takes 0.2 ms instead of 1.5 ms.
- After a respawn the recolor is bound to the new Helldiver at once, without a new recolor job.
- Fewer memory reads per frame: the Armory preview takes one read instead of two while it is shown, and the slot the game keeps for your body copies is read every other frame, in one read.
- The 2-second check no longer asks the engine about every recolored material (only after a change), and the 30-frame recolor check reads one texture per check whatever their number.
- v1.3's per-frame costs are measured offline only (call counts per frame pinned in the tests); the last real-play measurement is v1.2's.
- Checked on screen in the Armory on 31 pairs on both body types, from the front, both sides and the rear: the recolored part ends a median 4 to 6 CIEDE2000 from the other item's color in every view, and an independent review of the renders confirmed the fixes above; mission lighting is not yet checked.

# v1.2

- The Armory no longer stutters each time it recolors an item you hover: the mod refills its kept color textures instead of making new ones, which made the game wait for its renderer (one 7-15 ms frame per recolor in real play).
- New color textures are made only when none of the size is free, two spares at a time, so after the first recolors of a session none are made.
- New analyses go to the disk cache a few seconds after the last recolor while no preview is shown, spread over frames, instead of rewriting the whole file (up to 1.4 MB) on the frame of a recolor.
- Reading the disk cache at the first recolor of a session is spread over frames too.
- The log gives each recolor's apply time and how many textures it made.
- Played in joined missions and the Armory; measured in live play: 0.008 ms per frame in missions and 0.013 on the ship, and a frame over 2 ms in 3.5% of seconds on the ship and in the Armory, at most 5.6 ms (v1.1: 24-31%, mostly 7-15 ms).
- Known: the first new item of a session still opens the game's data in one frame (63 ms once in live play).

# v1.1

- First public release, renamed from Helmet Color Match: the log, the disk cache and the Mod Options Menu settings take the new name, so a test build's settings start from the defaults once.
- Only colors change now: every helmet and armor keeps its own material (metal, shine, fabric weave, wear, camo pattern), so fabric no longer turns to chrome or gold and shiny helmets stay shiny.
- Decals and patterns on recolored parts stay visible.
- Accents such as stripes and colored trims take the other item's accent color, even very small ones, or keep their own color when it has none.
- Painted patterns, such as the yellow stripes many helmets and armors share, take the other item's accent color too, and an item whose accent is a pattern passes that color on.
- A paint going to black, grey or white leaves no tint behind: its shading colors follow it instead of turning brown or teal.
- Every helmet and armor in the game was measured in the Armory, for which parts show, how much, and how each paint looks there; the mod ships that table and matches what you see.
- Parts the Armory never shows no longer count, so the Cinderblock's hidden lime and the cape mount's yellow no longer reach a helmet.
- Each part gets the color that makes it look like the other item, glossy and metal parts included, so the Salamander is as dark as the Doubt Killer's plates instead of lighter grey.
- Sheen and highlights no longer count as paint: armors matched to the Obedient stay dark instead of turning pale, and the Doubt Killer's glossy red trim gives red, not pink.
- An armor's main color is its most visible paint, its undersuit counting half, so the Fire Fighter gives its grey plates and never its tan padding.
- A dark armor with large light plates reads as those plates, so helmets matched to the Cinderblock are light grey like its chest plate.
- A small but visible emblem counts as an armor's accent, so the Cinderblock's orange reaches the Battle Master's stripes.
- A part that already matches one of the armor's main colors keeps it, so the Battle Master's light crest no longer turns black.
- The helmet's main part takes the armor's main color, a dark color goes to a dark part and a light one to a light part.
- An armor's undersuit black only goes to a helmet's dark parts now, so dark armors no longer turn white shells or grey panels black.
- A large area never takes a small trim color: a black cap with a gold band now gives a dark armor instead of a gold one.
- A dark but vivid red trim counts as an armor's accent, and a large muted part such as a tan hood is matched like a main part instead of taking the accent color.
- Armor and helmets released after the measurement keep the earlier model of what shows and how it looks.
- The measurement adds about 330 KB to the mod, read once at startup (about 1.5 ms offline); nothing runs per frame for it.
- Helmets and armor with large ID masks are analysed without a 2 MB buffer and with more pause points, for shorter frames while a new item is read.
- The disk cache also keeps the game's shared detail samples, so color matching needs no game file once an item was seen; older cache files are rebuilt once.
- The Armory's character view and the game's other UI previews of your Helldiver now show the matched colors too, including an item you hover; the Armory's thumbnails keep their original colors.
- Idle cost: one more 8-byte memory read every 4th frame to see whether such a preview is shown, and two reads every frame while it is.
- Tested offline on all 42,660 helmet and armor pairs and in the game on the ship and in the Armory (the sixteen pairs reported from play); not yet in a mission.
- Measured in real play: an earlier v1.1 build took 0.025 ms per frame on the ship, plus one frame of 7 to 15 ms each time the Armory preview recolors an item you hover; v1.0 took 0.012 ms per frame in missions.
- Steam build 25480438 only; requires Bingus Shared Loader v18 or newer.

# v1.0

- Recolors your helmet to match your armor, or your armor to match your helmet (Mod Options Menu: Color Matching).
- Colors come from the game's own files at runtime, so new armor and helmets work as released; nothing per item ships with the mod.
- Matching keeps visors, lenses, lights and skin as they are, keeps dark undersuits dark and copies whole paint rows (color, wear, metal, camo).
- Leaves complete sets (helmet and armor of the same set) unchanged unless you turn that off.
- Only your own Helldiver changes; other players never change, and they see your original colors.
- Remembers each helmet's and armor's analysis on disk (HelmetColorMatch.cache), so after the first time the recolor appears within a couple of frames of load-in.
- Puts the recolor back if the game ever restores an original color texture on your Helldiver.
- Idle cost: one memory read per frame (two with a preview slot), plus two reads every 30 frames; no allocation.
- Steam build 25480438 only; requires Bingus Shared Loader v18 or newer.
