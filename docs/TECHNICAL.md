# Match Your Colors: how it works

Steam build 25480438 (EXE 1.8.46015.0). Formerly Helmet Color Match (unpublished test builds). The research named
below (`research/`: the Python prototypes of the matcher, the game-data extracts, the measurement in the Armory)
stays out of this repository; `tests/fixtures/` carries its results for the parity tests.

## Colors come from the game's files

Armor and helmet colors are material LUTs: textures of 23 x N texels (R16G16B16A16_FLOAT), one row per paint
slot. A mesh's ID mask says which row each texel uses. The game has no tint setting, so a recolor is a new LUT.

For each kit the mod reads, from the kit's own archive in the game's slim data files (`data/bundles.nxa`, a DSAR
file of LZ4 chunks holding the DSAA index, and `data/bundles.NN.nxa`), its pieces' units, their materials, each
material's LUT and ID mask, plus the shared customization archive (detail and camo tilers, shared cape mounts).
A piece whose unit is in neither archive is left out; a missing ID mask gives uniform rows. This is the data scope
of the quality results below (Matching). The mod also ships one measurement per item (Measured appearance, below);
items released after it use the texture model.

Files are read with `CreateFileW`/`ReadFile` into the mod's own buffers, only while a recolor job runs.

| Step | Source | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Index | `bundles.nxa` | item records sorted by name (binary search), entries per item read on demand |
| Archive contents | archive main item | 72-byte header, 32-byte type entries, 80-byte file entries |
| Unit materials | unit main part +112 | material slot keys, then material hashes |
| Material textures | material main part +64 count, +136 keys, values | LUT slot `0x7e662968`, ID mask slot `0xb281e5f2`, pattern slot `0x81d4c49d` (default pattern), pattern mask slot `0x05a27dd5` |
| LUT | texture, mip 0 | half floats |
| Coverage | ID mask mip 0, RGBA8, 1-2 layers | mean of each texel's normalized weights (sum > 0.05) |
| Pattern | kit piece record +32 (else the material's default): 3 x 1 R16G16B16A16_FLOAT | texel 0 = sRGB color and mask layer in w (-1 = none), texels 1-2 = controls |
| Pattern coverage | pattern mask (R8 or RGBA8, red channel), mip 2 of the layer | mean of `clamp(100 x (x - 0.5), 0, 1)`, the shader's mask strength |
| Detail / camo samples | `5015467d918378bf` (BC7, mip 4), `f8b2508e99fb9f28` (RGBA8, mip 2) | first 512 texels per layer |

## Measured appearance

`src/appearance_data.lua` (generated by `research/make_appearance.py`, read by `src/appearance.lua`) holds, for each
of the 293 helmets and armors in the game, what the Armory's front view shows. Measured in the game
(`research/live/live_appearance.py`): the preview Helldiver held
still (the body's animation state machine off; the cape hidden, as its cloth keeps moving), every row of the item
coded in binary over the three color channels in complementary capture pairs (other materials black), so each screen
pixel names its row; per row its pixels (outside its piece's pattern), the light it shows (pixels x mean screen
luminance) and its response, captured with its own material at colors 0 and sRGB 0.25 and with matte grey for the
lighting: perceived = s + g x mean albedo per channel, relative to the light a matte albedo of 1 gets there (5,749
LUT rows, pooled over items by pixels). A row's gloss floor s loses the part of its look that only a few pixels carry:
the trimmed mean (25th-75th percentile) of its screen luminance against the mean, beyond the median row's skew (the
AC-2 Obedient's hood fabric, L 18 on screen, measured L 36 from the highlights on its folds). A kit counts as measured
when every LUT of its rows was measured (else the kit changed since, or the rows are another body's pieces).

What the measurement showed: the texture model counted parts that never show (the SR-64 Cinderblock's lime row,
1.6% of its texture, 0 pixels) and misweighted others (the I-92 Fire Fighter's tan padding: 11% of its texture, 4.8%
on screen); and paint looks differ from the model by more than the matching tolerances (matte rows -6 to +8 L, bare
metal 13 L darker with a 17 L spread, chroma about 0.8x).

## Matching

Matcher v12 (`src/matcher.lua` and `src/transfer.lua`, a port of `research/match12.py`; v1.0 used v10, the first
v1.1 builds v11). For a measured item it uses screen shares and perceived colors, keeping v11's rules:

- Shares are screen pixels; a pattern's share is its pixels. A row's perceived color is its response at its mean
  albedo, its gloss floor counted 1 - chroma / 20 (clipped): on neutral paint gloss reads as lightness (silver
  plates), on colored paint as highlights over the paint (the UF-84 Doubt Killer's glossy red trim looks coral on
  screen and is red paint). Rows group by model color (one paint stays one design color where it looks lighter or
  darker); a group's color is its rows' perceived colors, mean by pixels. Classes (dark neutral, structure, accent,
  vivid, visible) go by the model color, the thresholds the user's reports validated; colors (identity, matches,
  lightness relations, offsets, the fit) are perceived.
- An armor's identity: its most salient paint (share, undersuit 0.5, dark neutrals 0.5); when that looks dark
  (perceived L < 30, neutral) and a main color (share >= 0.15) that looks light shows more light, that color (the
  SR-64 Cinderblock reads as its light plates; 10 of 135 armors). Unmeasured items keep v11.5's dark weight 0.25.
- The anchor is the target's largest part (share >= 0.10) of the identity's look class (dark or not), else one that
  already matches it, else the largest: the helmet's main part takes the armor's main color (the FS-23 Battle
  Master's shell, not its smaller crest). A part that already matches one of the source's main colors (share >=
  0.10) keeps it.
- A measured source's accent counts from 0.1% of its pixels (the Cinderblock's orange emblem, 0.12%); texture shares
  from 0.5%.
- The fit (`src/transfer.lua`) reaches the desired perceived color through the row's own response, so a glossy or
  metal row lands where it looks right; a pattern's new texel color is the desired color through its measured gain.
  An unmeasured bare-metal row taking paint keeps v11.2's 0.04 paint-reflection lift.

Every pair the user reported (sixteen, 2026-10-05) is checked against the look agreed for it in
`tests/test_parity.lua`, with the lightness the Obedient's armors, the Cinderblock's helmets and the Salamander from
the Doubt Killer and the Fire Fighter must reach. Judged by what the Armory shows (`research/scratch/eval12.py`, all
21,330 helmet <- armor pairs before the second review: share of the target's visible paint further than CIEDE2000
10 from every source color): v12 90th percentile 13% against v11.5's 45%; the helmet's main part's lightness
against the armor's identity within -7 to +5 L (10th to 90th percentile) against -19 to +13 L.

### Matcher v11 (kept within v12)


- Each LUT row's perceived color: the shader's albedo path averaged over 512 detail texels, darkened by
  0.55 x metallic, in CIELAB.
- Rows are grouped by CIEDE2000 < 6 within one metal and camo class. Lens and light rows (mode >= 2.5) and skin
  pieces never change. Dark neutrals mostly from undergarments are structure. Pieces in the cape slot are left
  out: every armor carries one of two generic cape pieces (LUTs `a52382a8e96628da`, `1120e335578fdc12`) whose
  colors are not its design; their yellow rows were the accent of 58 of 135 armors (the CM-09 Bonesnapper's, with
  no yellow on it).
- The anchor takes the source's identity color (an armor's most salient paint: its undersuit area counts 0.5, dark
  neutrals 0.25; a helmet's largest paint): a target group that already matches it, else the largest of the
  identity's class (dark neutral or not), else the largest. A light identity therefore lands on a helmet's shell
  or hood, not on its dark mask. The other groups are placed by a local search over lightness and saturation
  relative to the anchor, colors
  already matched, source proportions and light/dark contrast. A large target area (share >= 0.15) takes only a
  main source color (share >= 0.10) or one it already matches, never a trim. A source's structure color (its
  undersuit) goes only to a dark neutral target group or one that already matches it.
- Identity weights: an armor's dark parts are many small pieces (straps, gloves, webbing, plate backs) whose
  texture area outweighs their visible area, and its plates define the look more than its undersuit. Dark 0.75
  (the first v1.1 builds) gave the TR-117 Alpha Commander (48% dark, 26% green plates), the CW-36 Winter Warrior
  (white) and the DS-191 Scorpion (beige) dark helmets; dark 0.5 still gave the CM-09 Bonesnapper (56% dark, 17%
  green undersuit, 16% cream plates) a dark one. Undersuit 0.5 and dark 0.25 is the one setting tried that gives
  each of these its visible main color (Alpha Commander green, Winter Warrior white, Scorpion beige, Bonesnapper
  cream, Doubt Killer and B-01 dark); per armor 97 of 127 identities match a helmet of the armor's own set (the
  most with other settings: 105), as the game's sets often pair a dark helmet with a colored armor.
- Accents (chroma >= 20, not the anchor; share up to 0.10, or up to 0.35 when vivid (chroma >= 35), or any share
  below the 2% pairing minimum) take the source's accent color: its most salient saturated color (chroma x
  sqrt(share)) with share >= 0.005 and lightness >= 30 (from 20 when vivid: the common red trim, L 29) among its
  color groups and its pattern colors, so a small yellow trim of a dark armor counts. They keep their own color
  when they are near-black (L < 20), already match a source color, or the source has no accent. A large muted
  part (the DS-10 Big Game Hunter's 26% tan hood) is paired like a main part.
- Patterns: a kit piece's pattern texture (the kit's, else the material's default) is drawn over the base color
  where the material's pattern mask is set (`research/ref/lut.frag`); 66 of 158 helmets and 57 of 135 armors have a
  saturated one, from 17 pattern textures (the yellow-khaki `f18dfbb5346732c1` is the common one). A pattern's
  share is its mask coverage x piece weight / materials over the paint area (helmets median 5.6%, armors 0.7%). A
  target pattern whose color is an accent (chroma >= 20, L >= 20) and matches neither a source color nor the
  source's accent takes the accent: a copy of the pattern texture with texel 0's color replaced (mask layer and
  controls kept), bound on the pattern slot where the vanilla pattern is bound and put back like the LUTs. The
  user's FS-23 Battle Master stripes and RS-67 Null Cipher's 0.9% marks stayed yellow against the UF-84 Doubt
  Killer's red before this.
- Each recolored row keeps every material column (mode, detail layer and controls, metallic, specular,
  roughness, camo controls, tiling, every w channel). Its colors (base, detail, wear, camo) follow the row's
  paint, the CIELAB of its mean albedo, to a new paint color (`src/transfer.lua`): on a saturated paint the colors
  of its hue family (within 60 degrees) rotate and scale with it and its neutral colors shift lightness only; on
  a neutral paint the colors near it take its CIELAB change; toward a neutral target every following color takes
  the target's tone and only lightness is fitted. Other colors stay (bare metal, dirt, details of another hue).
  The new paint color is fitted in at most 8 steps so the row's perceived color reaches its group's new color
  plus the row's own offset (its hue offset scaled by the chroma change). v1.0 copied the whole source row, which
  also copied the material and looked wrong on other meshes (fabric as chrome or gold, shiny shells as cloth,
  decals hidden by the copied detail roughness); the first v1.1 build moved every color by one CIELAB offset,
  which turned neutral detail colors teal when a red paint went neutral (a brown-looking armor).
- Bare metal taking paint: a paint shows about 4% white reflection that a bare metal (metallic >= 0.9) does not.
  A bare-metal row taking a paint color aims for 0.04 more linear light in each channel; everything else takes
  the color as it is. Measured in the Armory: the Salamander's metal faceplate fitted to the B-01's dark paint (both
  model L 21) showed L 15 on screen while the armor's plates showed L 29-34; the unchanged silver faceplate (model
  L 45) showed L 44. A build that also took 0.04 away from paint taking a bare-metal color turned paint black below
  L 23 (the FS-37 Ravager helmet from the PH-202 Twigsnapper's dark bronze).

All 21,330 pairs per direction (research, runtime data scope; foreign paint: share of the target's paint further
than CIEDE2000 10 from every source color of share >= 0.02 and from the source's accent, kept accents count):

| Metric (v11.4 in brackets) | helmet <- armor | armor <- helmet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| lenses untouched | every pair | every pair |
| foreign paint median, vanilla -> matched | 14.8% -> 0% | 20.6% -> 0.7% (1.3%) |
| foreign paint 90th percentile, matched | 1.3% (0%) | 5.3% (9.6%) |
| more foreign paint than vanilla, model colors | 21 (24) | 14 (11) |
| more foreign paint than vanilla, colors as seen | 57 (47) | 87 (76) |
| light and dark parts flattened to one lightness | 676 (649) | 1,255 (1,178) |
| accent groups taking the source's accent, of all | 4,148 of 8,100 (7,045) | 5,489 of 12,640 (7,442 of 14,062) |
| accent groups below the 2% pairing minimum taking it, of all | 1,912 of 2,700 (2,412) | 18,821 of 31,284 (22,509 of 40,922) |
| saturated target patterns taking it, of all | 2,645 of 4,455 (2,233) | 3,536 of 6,952 (3,228) |
| same-set pairs, median change in CIEDE2000 (v10) | 9.3 (9.8, v10 13.6) | 8.7 (9.7, v10 10.3) |

"As seen" adds the paint's 0.04 reflection to every color that is not bare metal. Worse than vanilla are mostly
metal rows asked for a bright gold or white, which they cannot reach (the metal darkening of the model; they keep
the brightest metal of that hue). Accents and patterns keep their color when the source has no accent or they
already match one of its colors; fewer accents translate than in v11.4 because 58 armors' only "accent" was the
generic cape piece's yellow. The first v1.1 builds (dark weight 0.75, no undersuit rule) had 180 and 21 pairs with
parts turned near-black, 538 and 1,223 flattened, same-set change 7.9 and 8.8.

Every pair the user reported (twelve, 2026-10-05) is checked against the look agreed for it in
`tests/test_parity.lua` (target color -> source color and kind, and the Battle Master's pattern).

The Lua port equals the research pipeline (float64) on every kit's rows and patterns, on all 42,660 plans and
pattern plans (6,180 of them change a pattern) and on the fitted colors of every planned row of 287 sampled plans
(1e-7) (`tests/test_parity.lua`).

## Local player only

| Step | Memory (game.dll) |
| --- | --- |
| Local player | player manager `[+0x3326468]`: entity records `+232 + 8i`, flags `+20` bit 0 = owned by this machine |
| Player index, Helldiver | map `+208` (entity id -> index), unit `+936 + 32 * index`, peer `+712 + 56 * index` |
| Applied kits | customization manager `[+0x33264F8]`: map `+2352`, record `+2412 + 68r` (body, helmet, cape, armor) |
| Avatar entity | entities `[+0x346BF98]`: map `+0xF22EC8` (unit -> entity), record `+0xF32F18 + 24e` (avatar resource, local bit) |
| Unit arrays | avatars `[+0x3326D20]`: map `+248`, record `+5532272 + 440 * index`, units `+220` (armor), `+260` (undergarment), `+300` (accessory), by slot |
| Body copies | `[+0x346D580]`: slot `+2080 + 2184s` whose peer is the player's, entries `+68 + 132n`, units at `+12` (gameplay copies: static research places their filling in death and disconnect code, not a UI preview) |
| UI preview | UiPreviewSystem `[+0x347CE60]`: slot L = `u32 [player manager + 948 + 32 * index]`, record `+49160 + 160 * L`: units `+12/+52/+92` (the avatar layout), kits shown `+136` body, `+140` armor, `+144` helmet; occupied count and mask `+50440/+50444` (`src/preview.lua`) |

The helmet is slot 0 of the three arrays, the armor slots 1-9. A target unit's materials are rebound only when
their LUT is one the plan changes: the vanilla texture object comes from `ResourceManagerApi.get_by_id`
(`get_engine_api(5)` +0x40, after `can_get_by_id` +0x30). Other players' units, body copies and UI preview slots
are never touched.

The UI preview (the Armory's character view and the game's other UI previews of the local player) can show
another pair than the one equipped (a hovered item): it is a second target with its own job, textures and
bindings, planned for the kits its record shows. Checked in the game on 2026-10-05 (solo: slot 0): the Armory's
front view recolored within one to three frames of each change, a hovered helmet included (jobs 0.6-4.2 ms of
work, no frame over 1.5 ms). The game spawns a hovered item's units in its update after the
mods' update, so a newly hovered item shows its original colors for one frame (more when the item was never
analysed). The Armory's thumbnails (UiPreviewSystem slots 4-7) are not recolored: whether a recolor reaches the
captured image depends on timing that is not checked yet.

## Engine calls

Script C API `[game.dll + 0x3326308]`: Unit (+24) `alive` +1824, `num_meshes` +424, `mesh` +440; Mesh (+232)
`num_materials` +8, `material` +24, `commit` +32; Material (+40) `set_resource` +128. `get_engine_api`
(exe `0xa4e50`, prefix checked): 26 = RenderBufferApi (`create_buffer` +0x60, `destroy_buffer` +0x70,
`lookup_resource` +0x78). Every function address is compared with its expected executable offset once per
session before any call. A runtime LUT is RGBA32F, 23 x N, one mip; its data stays referenced while it lives.

Texture lifetime: a plan's textures live until the plan changes (other kit, direction, Off) or the addon pauses or
stops. Then the vanilla texture goes back on every live tracked material (only where the material still holds
the mod's texture), and the textures are destroyed four frames later.

## Analysis cache

`src/cache.lua`: `MatchYourColors.cache` in the mod's log folder keeps the shared detail and camo tiler samples
(512 texels per layer as bytes) and every analysed kit (rows and pattern areas as `%.17g` text, LUTs and pattern
textures as raw float32), at most 64
entries, most recently used first. The first line names the format version (5), the game build and the slim
index (chunk count and size); an entry's key holds the kit id, body type, archive and a checksum of the kit's
piece list. Anything unexpected discards the whole file (an older file is rebuilt once). The file is read on the
first job of a session and written after a job that read the game files, through a temporary file and a rename.
With it, a session's first recolor needs no game-file read, the color transfer included (v1.0 live: 2 frames,
2.2 ms of work).

## Recolor check

Every 30 frames the mod reads the LUT (or pattern) binding of one recolored material per runtime texture (two reads
each, through pointer objects kept per address: no allocation). If the game has put that material's original back
while the unit lives, the recolor is applied again and a line is logged. No such event was seen in testing.

## Per-frame cost

| Frame | Calls | In game |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Idle, recolor applied | 1 `read_into` (120 bytes of unit refs); +1 with a preview slot; +1 while it has entries | unmeasured in game; about 1-2 us per read from the reference table |
| Idle, UI preview not shown | +1 `read_into` (8 bytes) on 1 frame in 4 | unmeasured in game |
| UI preview shown | +2 `read_into` per frame (8 bytes, 160 bytes); a change of its kits starts a job for that pair | unmeasured in game |
| Every 30 frames | +2 `read_into` per runtime texture (1 for the helmet, up to 10 for armor, +1 per changed pattern; the UI preview's too while shown) | unmeasured in game |
| No local Helldiver | 1 resolve (up to about 20 reads) every 15 frames, nothing between | unmeasured in game |
| Every 120 frames | the local-player chain again (about 20 reads), `alive` per tracked binding | unmeasured in game |
| Change | the chain, then a job: 1.5 ms of work per frame, ended at the first pause point after it (2 `QueryPerformanceCounter` per frame plus one per pause point) | job length and longest frame logged per job |
| Apply | per target unit: engine calls to list materials, two reads per material; per changed LUT: `can_get_by_id`, `get_by_id`, `create_buffer`, `lookup_resource`; `set_resource` per material, `commit` per mesh | one frame of 7-15 ms per Armory preview recolor in real play (v1.1, inferred from counts) |

Idle frames allocate nothing (`tests/test_idle_alloc.lua`, interpreter only). No `VirtualQuery`, no memory
writes, no page-protection changes. Call counts per frame kind are pinned in `tests/test_addon.lua`.

Jobs, measured inside the game in harness sessions (30 FPS cap; these are not real-play frame costs): first
analysis of a helmet and armor 85.6 ms of work over 50 frames, longest frame 2.1 ms, 207 file reads (12 MB, longest
0.7 ms); a new helmet later 11 ms over 7 frames; switching direction 0.4 ms; from the cache 2.2 ms over 2 frames.

Real play with v1.0 (study `real-play-newmods1`, mod log): a new item took 7 to 45 ms of work over 4 to 25 frames;
the two armors with 512 x 512 ID masks (CM-14 Physician, B-24 Enforcer) took 181 ms over 37 frames (longest frame
22.3 ms) and 64 ms over 31 frames (8.7 ms). The same analyses in the game's `lua51.dll` outside the game take 54
and 40 ms with no stretch between pause points over 1.75 ms, so in the game the work runs about 3x slower and
frames also carry garbage-collector work the job's allocations trigger. The game's JIT cache did not flush
(limits 64 MB / 8000 traces). v1.1 changes the reading:

- ID-mask coverage reads blocks of rows (at most 128 KB) instead of whole layers (2 MB for a 512 x 512 two-layer
  mask); the reader keeps 3 decoded chunks, so alternating layer reads decode each chunk once (9 decodes per such
  mask, as before).
- The LZ4 decoder pauses every 32 KB of output (a chunk is up to 256 KB).
- Outside the game: longest stretch between pause points 1.29 / 0.74 ms for those two armors (was 1.75 / 1.15 ms);
  first-job allocation 7.6 MB (was 10.1 MB): reader 1.3 MB, shared archive table of contents 0.9 MB, shared
  samples and buffers 2.8 MB, the Physician's analysis 2.7 MB (was 4.7 MB).
- Color transfer (new): up to 5.2 ms of work per plan in the game's `lua51.dll` outside the game (54 rows of the
  DS-191 Scorpion), longest row 0.63 ms; a pause point after each row.

Real play with v1.1 before matcher v12 (study `real-play-newmods2`, two launches on the ship): 0.025 and 0.051 ms
per frame; each job's longest slice 0.1 to 3.6 ms (one 9.9 ms slice building the kit catalogue at the first
recolor). The Armory preview is recolored for every item you hover there, and each recolor has one frame of 7 to 15
ms (up to 32.5 ms) outside the sliced job, most likely the apply (render buffers created, materials rebound, meshes
committed; inferred from counts, not timed per step): 24 to 31% of the measured seconds had one. Matcher
v12's measurement table, read once at startup (about 1.5 ms outside the game), is unmeasured in game.

The game-data reader and its buffers exist only while a job reads files. Reusing them across a burst of jobs
(browsing the Armory) would save about 2 MB of allocation per job; not done yet.

Compiled code: only the hot loops (LZ4, ID-mask coverage, color sampling) and the frame step compile; everything
else is kept interpreted with `jit.off(fn, true)`. Live: 36 KB of machine code, 99 traces in a harness session; 48 KB,
139 traces in real play (v1.0; the first build compiled 301 KB, 419 traces); 49 KB, 183 traces on the ship with
v1.1 before matcher v12.
